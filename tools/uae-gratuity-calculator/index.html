How gratuity is usually estimated

Start with your last basic salary

Gratuity estimates are usually based on your basic salary, not the full salary package.

Work out the daily basic salary

A simple estimator divides the monthly basic salary by 30 days to get a daily amount.

Apply gratuity days by service length

A common estimator uses 21 days per year for the first five years and 30 days per year after that.

Review resignation or termination context

The final settlement discussion can differ depending on how the employment ends, so this page explains that context beneath the estimate.

Resignation vs termination

If you resigned

Your final outcome can depend on your employment terms and how your employer handles the settlement. Use this calculator as a planning estimate, then confirm the final figure with HR.

  • Good for pre-resignation planning
  • Useful when comparing offers or relocation choices
  • Best confirmed with your company before notice ends
If you were terminated

The estimate still starts from basic salary and service length, but your final settlement may also include notice pay, unused leave, or deductions depending on your case.

  • Helpful for settlement expectations
  • Can be compared with your final HR statement
  • Does not include leave salary or notice pay automatically

Frequently asked questions

Is UAE gratuity based on total salary or basic salary?

For quick estimates, people usually use the last basic salary. Housing, transport, and other allowances are generally not the number users enter into a basic gratuity estimator.

Can I calculate gratuity from dates instead of typing service years?

Yes. This page uses joining and leaving dates so the service period feels more real and reduces guesswork.

Does the calculator include unused leave salary, notice pay, or deductions?

No. This page focuses on gratuity only. Those extra items can affect your final settlement, but they are separate from the estimate shown here.

Can I share the result with HR?

Yes. Use the copy button to paste the estimate into email, notes, or a chat with HR. It is best used as a discussion starting point rather than a final legal statement.
